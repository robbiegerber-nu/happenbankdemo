How the limits and signals work
  • Center line: the metric's level in the baseline period (shaded). Limits: the center plus or minus the number of standard deviations (σ) you choose. Lower settings catch smaller changes but raise more false alarms.
  • Percentages such as service level use a Laney p′ chart. Its limits move with each day's volume and with the day-to-day variation seen in the baseline.
  • Times, counts, NPS, adherence, occupancy and staffing use an individuals (XmR) chart, with limits from the average change between neighboring points.
  • Outside the limits (red-orange diamond): a sharp break. Run rules (amber ring): 8 points in a row on one side of the center, 6 in a row rising or falling, or 2 of 3 beyond 2σ on the same side. The ring marks the point that completes a pattern and any that continue it.
  • By category asks whether each category's result for the whole period is further from the whole-center result than its own day-to-day variation explains. A real, lasting difference will show as a signal.
  • Limits are not targets. They show what the process normally does, so targets are left off these charts. A metric can be stable and still miss its target.
  • Days of the week differ, which can create daily patterns, so charts open on weekly data. Weeks start on Sunday and only complete weeks are shown. Closed days are skipped.
  • Small samples are hollow grey and left out of limits and signals. At least 20 usable points are needed; a shorter baseline is extended back to reach 20.
